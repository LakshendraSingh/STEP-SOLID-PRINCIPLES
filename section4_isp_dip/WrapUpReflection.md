# Section 4.5 — Wrap-up Reflection on the 4-Lab SOLID Journey

## 4-Lab Journey Reflection:
If management presents a new requirement such as **"add a MongoAccountRepository"** (or **"add a WhatsAppNotificationService"**), the refactored architecture allows us to fulfill it with near-zero friction. Today, we would simply create one brand-new class (`MongoAccountRepository.java` implementing `AccountRepository` or `WhatsAppNotificationService.java` implementing `NotificationService`) and touch only the top-level application composition root (`Main.java` or Spring configuration) where dependencies are injected. In contrast, under the monolithic `BankAccount` design from Lab 1, adding MongoDB persistence would have required cracking open `BankAccount.java` itself, modifying its internal fields, altering the `saveToDatabase()` method, risking unintended side effects in core deposit/withdrawal logic, and invalidating existing unit tests. Thanks to the SOLID principles (SRP, OCP, LSP, ISP, and DIP), high-level business rules are completely shielded from low-level storage and delivery mechanisms. This achieves maximum cohesion, loose coupling, effortless extensibility, and rock-solid testability across the entire enterprise banking system.
